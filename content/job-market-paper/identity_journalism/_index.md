abstract: "Does it matter who writes the news? I study this question by looking at gender differences in reporting on violence against women in Italy. Matching the universe of murders with female victims in 2006--2022 to newspaper articles, I construct indexes for two reporting narratives: victim-centered narratives, which focus on the victim and the violence she experienced and point to support resources, and perpetrator-centered narratives, which focus on motives such as jealousy or a fit of rage. I show that female journalists use more victim-centered and fewer perpetrator-centered narratives, exploiting the conditionally random assignment of reporters to crimes within newsrooms to account for selection in the stories covered. Combining reporter assignment with province-level readership in a staggered difference-in-differences design, I show that greater exposure to female reporters after a murder raises calls to the national helpline for violence against women. An online experiment sheds light on the mechanism: victim-centered narratives shift norms around help-seeking. It also reveals potential engagement costs, as these narratives decrease engagement among men but help retain female readers. The characteristics of those who report the news are thus a relevant supply-side determinant of both news content and reader behavior."
